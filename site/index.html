One episode, and then it is everywhere

You add an episode. FOSSCast does the rest of it without being asked.

  1. Your page is written

    An episode page with your notes, your chapters and a player, and the show's front page updated, both plain HTML that a search engine can read.

  2. The feed goes out

    Full iTunes namespace, Podcasting 2.0 transcripts, chapters, people and funding, real file sizes and durations. A feed check tells you what Apple and Spotify still want.

  3. AppleSpotifyOvercastPocket CastsAntennaPod

    The apps come and get it

    Every podcast app pulls the feed on its own schedule. Your listeners get the episode without doing anything, wherever they already listen.

  4. You see what happened

    Months as bars, days as a curve, a heatmap of when people listen, and how long an episode keeps earning. Counted on your server, from numbers that cannot be joined back to a person.

Counted, never followed

There is no analytics service in the loop, no third-party font, no script from anybody else, and no cookie for a listener. The charts are drawn on your own machine from counters that hold a total and nothing else.

A download means the audio reached a device. It does not mean anybody listened, because podcast apps fetch new episodes automatically for everyone subscribed. Apple and Spotify can tell you more than that, and it is worth knowing why: they wrote the app, so they can watch what happens inside it, and they can only see the listening that happens there. That is the whole reason they want your show heard in their app and nowhere else. Anybody who simply serves a file, this software included, sees a request and nothing after it.

So the number here says downloads, because downloads is what it counts.

All of it, free, on your own machine

There is no self-hosted edition and a better one. This is the product.

Your hosts, properly introduced

Every host gets a photo, a role and a write-up: a card on your Hosts page and a page of their own. Two of you or twenty, in the order you choose. They travel in the feed as well, so apps can put a face to a voice.

Getting paid

Patreon, Buy Me a Coffee, Ko-fi, Liberapay, GitHub Sponsors, Open Collective and PayPal. Paste your page, get a button on your site and a funding tag in the feed. No account yet? Each one has a sign-up link beside it.

Bring your back catalogue

Paste your current RSS feed and every episode comes across with its metadata and its identity, GUIDs and all, so directories treat the move as the same show rather than a new one.

Media anywhere

Upload to your own server or point at files hosted elsewhere, archive.org included. Both are first class. Range requests are handled properly, so players seek instantly.

Fits the server you already have

The bundled Caddy is optional, never assumed. nginx, Apache, a Cloudflare Tunnel, a tailnet-only instance behind Tailscale: all supported, all documented, all tested.

One instance, one podcast

A decision, not a shortcut. One show per instance keeps the whole thing small enough to read in an afternoon and to trust with your audience. Running three shows? Run three instances. They are tiny.

And the small things, which are most of the work

  • Editing saves itself: podcast details, hosts, episodes, design
  • Deleting always takes a second, deliberate click
  • Drafts and dated episodes stay hidden until their moment
  • Seasons, trailers and bonus episodes, properly tagged
  • An embeddable player for any episode
  • Chapters typed as plain lines of text
  • Password reset by email, or from the command line
  • A read-only demo mode for showing the thing off
  • Every uploaded image gets a small web copy made for it
  • Pages are plain HTML and one stylesheet: no framework, no build step

Questions people actually ask

What does it cost to run?

Nothing beyond the machine. FOSSCast is GPL v3 and always will be. A small VPS handles a podcast comfortably; bandwidth is the only thing that scales with your audience.

Can I leave?

Yes, and it is deliberately easy. Your episodes are files on your disk and your data is plain JSON beside them. Your feed is a standard RSS feed with a stable GUID, so moving elsewhere is a DNS change and a redirect.

Do my listeners get tracked?

No. There is no analytics service, no third-party font, no script from anyone else, and no cookie for a listener. Download counts come from your own server logs, by episode, not by person.

Do I need to know Docker?

You need to be able to paste a file and run one command. If that already sounds like a bad evening, the hosted option exists precisely for you.

Is video supported?

Yes. Episodes can be audio or video, hosted here or anywhere reachable by URL, and the page picks the right player.

Where does live streaming fit?

It does not, and that is deliberate. FOSSCast is the published show. Recording and going live are a different job for a different tool, so your public site stays small, fast and hard to break.

How do I update it?

Two commands, and your data is in a named volume that is left alone: docker compose pull then docker compose up -d.

Two minutes, if you have a machine

Linux, Docker, a domain pointed at it, ports 80 and 443 open. Paste this into docker-compose.yml, change the three lines at the top, and run docker compose up -d. The certificate arrives on its own and you log in at /admin.

No checkout, no build step, no .env, no Caddyfile. The bundled Caddy is optional: delete it and point nginx, Apache, a Cloudflare Tunnel or Tailscale at the app instead.
