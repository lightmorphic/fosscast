Your podcast on your own
server, by tonight.

A page for every episode, a page for every host, a feed every app accepts, and colours you pick yourself. It is one file and one command. Nothing it serves ever calls anybody else.

Then: docker compose up -d

Linux, Docker, a domain pointed at the machine, ports 80 and 443 open. The certificate arrives on its own.

Claims, and how to check them yourself

Every line below is something a marketing page would simply assert. Run the command instead.

It brings no dependencies with it

Not a small number. None. The whole app is the standard library and 7,171 lines you could read in an afternoon.

$ jq .dependencies server/package.json
{}

Nothing it serves calls anybody else

No tracker, no font from Google, no script from a CDN, no cookie for a listener. Your audience is watched by nobody, including us, because we are not in the request.

$ curl -s https://demo.fosscast.org/ \
    | grep -cE 'src="https?://|@import|googleapis'
0

It is free, and stays free

GPL v3, which is the kind of free that cannot be taken back later. There is no paid tier of the software and no feature waiting behind a subscription.

$ head -2 LICENSE
GNU GENERAL PUBLIC LICENSE
Version 3, 29 June 2007

It is tested, and small

A hundred and thirty-one tests, no framework, run with the one that ships with Node. The image is 109 MB and carries the app and everything it serves.

$ cd server && npm test
# tests 131
# pass 131
# fail 0

What you get, all of it, free

There is no self-hosted edition and a better one. This is the product.

A website that looks like your show, not like ours

The Look tab hands the design to you. Fourteen preset colours or any hex code you like. Every other shade, light and dark, is worked out from the one you pick, including a link colour checked for contrast so nothing you choose can end up unreadable.

  • Backgrounds plain, one colour, a gradient at any angle, or your own image with dim, blur and tiling
  • Cards solid, outlined or glass; corners from hard square to fully round
  • Five typefaces, three page widths, three episode layouts, four cover sizes
  • Force light or dark, or follow the visitor; hide the switch if you like
  • Your own tagline and footer line, and a custom CSS box for anything else
  • A live preview of your real front page, saving as you go. There is no Save button

Statistics without surveillance

Months as bars, days as a curve, doughnuts for apps and countries, a heatmap of when people actually listen, and how long an episode keeps earning. Every chart is drawn on your own server from counters that cannot be joined back to a person. No charting library, no script, nobody else in the loop.

Feeds that get accepted first time

Full iTunes namespace, Podcasting 2.0 transcripts, chapters, people and funding, real file sizes and durations. A feed check in the dashboard lists exactly what Apple, Spotify and the rest look for, and what is still missing.

Your hosts, properly introduced

Every host gets a photo, a role and a write-up: a card on your Hosts page and a page of their own. Two of you or twenty, in the order you choose. They travel in the feed as well, so apps can put a face to a voice.

Getting paid

Patreon, Buy Me a Coffee, Ko-fi, Liberapay, GitHub Sponsors, Open Collective and PayPal. Paste your page, get a button on your site and a podcast:funding tag in the feed. No account yet? Each one has a sign-up link beside it.

Bring your back catalogue

Paste your current RSS feed and every episode comes across with its metadata and its identity, GUIDs and all, so directories treat the move as the same show rather than a new one.

Media anywhere

Upload to your own server or point at files hosted elsewhere, archive.org included. Both are first class. Range requests are handled properly, so players seek instantly.

Fits the server you already have

The bundled Caddy is optional, never assumed. nginx, Apache, a Cloudflare Tunnel, a tailnet-only instance behind Tailscale: all supported, all documented, all tested.

And the small things, which are most of the work

  • Editing saves itself: podcast details, hosts, episodes, design
  • Deleting always takes a second, deliberate click
  • Drafts and dated episodes stay hidden until their moment
  • Seasons, trailers and bonus episodes, properly tagged
  • An embeddable player for any episode
  • Chapters typed as plain lines of text
  • Password reset by email, or from the command line
  • A read-only demo mode for showing the thing off
  • Every uploaded image gets a small web copy made for it
  • Pages are plain HTML and one stylesheet: no framework, no build step

One instance, one podcast

This is a decision, not a limitation we have not got round to fixing. A single show per instance keeps the whole thing small enough to read in an afternoon and to trust with your audience, and it means the free edition is the entire product rather than the cheap half of a multi-tenant system you are paying for in bugs. Running three shows? Run three instances. They are tiny.

The same reasoning keeps live streaming out. FOSSCast is the published show. Recording and going live are a different job for a different tool, so your public site stays small, fast and hard to break.

Questions people actually ask

What does it cost to run?

Nothing beyond the machine. FOSSCast is GPL v3 and always will be. A small VPS handles a podcast comfortably; bandwidth is the only thing that scales with your audience.

Can I leave?

Yes, and it is deliberately easy. Your episodes are files on your disk and your data is plain JSON beside them. Your feed is a standard RSS feed with a stable GUID, so moving elsewhere is a DNS change and a redirect.

Do my listeners get tracked?

No. There is no analytics service, no third-party font, no script from anyone else, and no cookie for a listener. Download counts come from your own server logs, by episode, not by person.

Do I need to know Docker?

You need to be able to paste a file and run one command. If that already sounds like a bad evening, the hosted option exists precisely for you.

Is video supported?

Yes. Episodes can be audio or video, hosted here or anywhere reachable by URL, and the page picks the right player.

How do I update it?

Two commands, and your data is in a named volume that is left alone: docker compose pull then docker compose up -d.

If you would rather not run a server

Castmorphic runs this same software for you, the identical public code rather than a special edition, with none of the server work. Your own domain, updates and backups looked after, and somebody to ask when something looks wrong.

Your audio, your artwork and your listeners stay yours, and you can take the whole thing with you whenever you like. It is plain files and a standard feed, and this page tells you how to run it yourself for nothing, which is the point.
