What you get

Everything below is in the free, self-hosted edition. There is no paid tier of the software, and no feature waiting behind a subscription.

New

A website that looks like your show, not like ours

The Look tab hands the design to you. Fourteen preset colours or any hex code you like — and every other shade, in light mode and dark, is worked out from the one you pick, including a link colour checked for contrast so nothing you choose can end up unreadable.

  • Backgrounds: plain, a colour, a gradient at any angle, or your own image with dim, blur and tiling
  • Cards solid, outlined or glass; corners from hard square to fully round
  • Five typefaces, three page widths, three episode layouts, four cover sizes
  • Force light or dark, or follow the visitor; hide the switch if you like
  • Your own tagline and footer line, and a custom CSS box for anything else
  • A live preview of your real front page, and it saves as you go — there is no Save button
New

Your hosts, properly introduced

Every host gets a photo, a role and a write-up: a card on your Hosts page and a page of their own. Two of you or twenty, in the order you choose. They travel in the feed as well, so apps can put a face to a voice.

New

Getting paid

Patreon, Buy Me a Coffee, Ko-fi, Liberapay, GitHub Sponsors, Open Collective and PayPal. Paste your page, get a button on your site and a podcast:funding tag in the feed. No account yet? Each one has a sign-up link beside it.

Feeds that get accepted first time

Full iTunes namespace, Podcasting 2.0 transcripts, chapters, people and funding, real file sizes and durations. A feed check in the dashboard lists exactly what Apple, Spotify and the rest look for, and what is still missing.

Bring your back catalogue

Paste your current RSS feed and every episode comes across with its metadata and its identity — GUIDs and all — so directories treat the move as the same show rather than a new one.

Media anywhere

Upload to your own server or point at files hosted elsewhere, archive.org included. Both are first class. Range requests are handled properly, so players seek instantly.

Nothing phones home

No trackers, no third-party fonts or scripts, no cookies for listeners. Download counts are worked out on your own server without following anybody around. Even a custom CSS box has its off-site requests stripped.

Fits the server you already have

The bundled Caddy is optional, never assumed. nginx, Apache, a Cloudflare Tunnel, a tailnet-only instance behind Tailscale: all supported, all documented, all tested.

Fast by default

Every uploaded image gets a small web copy made for it while the full-size original goes to the directories. Pages are plain HTML and one stylesheet: no framework, no build step, no bundle.

The small things

  • Editing saves itself — podcast details, hosts, episodes, design
  • Deleting always takes a second, deliberate click
  • Drafts and dated episodes stay hidden until their moment
  • Seasons, trailers and bonus episodes, properly tagged
  • An embeddable player for any episode
  • Chapters typed as plain lines of text
  • Password reset by email, or from the command line
  • A read-only demo mode for showing the thing off

By design

One instance, one podcast

FOSSCast runs a single show per instance, on purpose. It keeps the whole thing small enough to read in an afternoon and to trust with your audience, and it means the free edition is the entire product: no multi-tenant complexity you are paying for in bugs. Running three shows? Run three instances; they are tiny.

Hosted

Or let us run it for you

Same software — the identical public code, not a special edition — with none of the server work. Your own machine, your own domain, updates and backups looked after, and someone to ask when something looks wrong.

Your audio, your artwork and your listeners stay yours, and you can take the whole thing with you whenever you like: it is plain files and a standard feed.

Ask about hosting

Questions people actually ask

What does it cost to run?

Nothing beyond the machine. FOSSCast is GPL v3 and always will be. A small VPS handles a podcast comfortably; bandwidth is the only thing that scales with your audience.

Can I leave?

Yes, and it is deliberately easy. Your episodes are files on your disk and your data is plain JSON beside them. Your feed is a standard RSS feed with a stable GUID, so moving elsewhere is a DNS change and a redirect.

Do my listeners get tracked?

No. There is no analytics service, no third-party font, no script from anyone else, and no cookie for a listener. Download counts come from your own server logs, by episode, not by person.

Do I need to know Docker?

You need to be able to paste a file and run one command. If that already sounds like a bad evening, the hosted option exists precisely for you.

Is video supported?

Yes. Episodes can be audio or video, hosted here or anywhere reachable by URL, and the page picks the right player.

Where does live streaming fit?

It does not, and that is deliberate. FOSSCast is the published show; recording and going live are a separate job for a separate tool, so your public site stays small, fast and hard to break.

Installing

The whole installation is this file

A Linux machine with Docker, a domain pointed at it, ports 80 and 443 open. Paste this into docker-compose.yml, edit the block at the top — everything you need to change is up there, and the domain is typed once — then run docker compose up -d.

docker-compose.yml 
x-config: &config
  DOMAIN: podcast.example.com          # your public address
  ADMIN_EMAIL: you@example.com         # the first admin account
  ADMIN_PASSWORD: change-me-to-something-long
  PUBLISHER_TOKEN: ""                  # optional, for the publish API

services:
  app:
    image: ghcr.io/lightmorphic/fosscast:latest
    restart: unless-stopped
    environment: *config
    volumes:
      - fosscast_data:/data
    read_only: true
    tmpfs: [/tmp]
    cap_drop: [ALL]
    security_opt: [no-new-privileges:true]

  caddy:
    image: caddy:2-alpine
    restart: unless-stopped
    environment: *config
    command: sh -c 'caddy reverse-proxy --from "$$DOMAIN" --to app:3100'
    ports: ["80:80", "443:443"]
    volumes:
      - caddy_data:/data
      - caddy_config:/config
    cap_add: [NET_BIND_SERVICE]

volumes:
  fosscast_data:
  caddy_data:
  caddy_config:

No checkout, no build, no .env, no Caddyfile. The image carries the app and everything it serves; the HTTPS certificate arrives on its own. Log in at /admin with the email and password you put in the file.

Updating

docker compose pull
docker compose up -d

Your data lives in a named volume and is left alone.

Already running a proxy?

Delete the caddy service, publish the app on 127.0.0.1:3100, and point nginx, Apache, a Cloudflare Tunnel or Tailscale Serve at it. FOSSCast never assumes it owns ports 80 and 443.

It is the same image the maintainer's own shows run on, built from main on every push. What you install is what we use.
